01

Executive read.

tareeq · compass

The career discovery space splits into four lanes: psychometric assessments (CareerExplorer, YouScience), school pathway platforms (Unifrog), AI strengths and job-matching (Mentra, PathwayU), and end-to-end pathway apps (Yenza, ISMOJO). Each of them solves one stage well. None of them solves the full arc — assessment, local pathway, weekly action, family context — for a young Muslim-majority audience whose decisions are shaped as much by elders and community as by the individual.

Tareeq's position is not another career test. It is a guided pathway app that turns CORE assessment results into local opportunities, mentor and counselor support, and weekly action steps a 16-year-old can actually take this week. The product surface we already ship — the editorial onboarding, the compass-as-progress mechanic, the calm night surface, Kai as a non-coercive guide — is the foundation. The competitive opportunity is to extend that arc past the assessment into the parts of the journey that don't yet exist in any single competitor's product.

Closest competitor by lane

  • End-to-end pathway model · Yenza Careers — the most structurally similar to where Tareeq is heading. Assessment → career match → action plan, served on the African continent. Their model validates the thesis; Tareeq's edge is MENA cultural fluency, Arabic-first voice, and family-aware framing.
  • Regional / MENA-adjacent signal · ISMOJO — operates in the same cultural orbit, builds school partnerships, and proves there's demand. Their gap: thin on assessment depth and on the editorial / mentorship layer.
  • Schools go-to-market · Unifrog and YouScience — institutional moats, deep counselor tooling. If Tareeq sells into schools, these set the bar. The opening: the consumer-grade experience neither of them delivers to the student themselves.
  • Free consumer funnel · CareerExplorer — strongest SEO and content-led acquisition. They convert curiosity into a 16-minute assessment and a paid upgrade. Tareeq can match their funnel and out-deliver their post-assessment experience.
  • Strengths-aware matching inspiration · Mentra — built for neurodivergent talent. Useful template for how Tareeq frames difference as fit rather than deficit.

The wedge

No competitor is solving for the "after". Every product in this set hands the user back a report, a cluster, or a match — and then ends. Tareeq's defensible product is what happens in the seven days after the assessment: a local opportunity surfaced, a mentor or counselor connection offered, a small action proposed and completed, and a parent-friendly summary the family can read together. That arc is the moat.

Yenza Careers

yenzacareers.com
Closest analogue · End-to-end pathway

Audience

Youth and young professionals across the African continent (16–25).

What they sell

A guided arc: assessment → career match → structured action plan → editorial content library. Direct-to-student.

Strengths

Coherent product narrative, strong African distribution, clear "after the test" arc, content depth.

Gaps Tareeq can exploit

Not built for MENA cultural context, not Arabic-first, no family/elder framing, no mentor pairing.

Tareeq takeaway: Yenza proves the end-to-end pathway thesis. Win by being the version built for Arabic-speaking and Muslim-majority youth, with family-aware framing and a mentor layer Yenza doesn't yet ship.

ISMOJO

ismojo.com
Closest regional signal · MENA-adjacent

Audience

Students and schools, with regional / MENA-adjacent positioning.

What they sell

Career exploration tooling, school partnerships, light assessment functionality. B2B-leaning go-to-market.

Strengths

Regional credibility, school relationships, validated demand in the territories Tareeq targets.

Gaps Tareeq can exploit

Thin on assessment depth, no editorial / mentorship layer, limited consumer-grade product polish.

Tareeq takeaway: ISMOJO confirms the regional demand and the school channel works. Tareeq's edge is product quality: the assessment depth, the editorial onboarding, and the Kai mentor voice are all surfaces ISMOJO doesn't compete on.

Unifrog

unifrog.org
School pathway platform

Audience

Schools, counselors, students 14–18. Heavy institutional play.

What they sell

A "destinations" platform — universities, apprenticeships, jobs, gap years — plus counselor dashboards and tools.

Strengths

Deep counselor tooling, comprehensive destination database, strong school distribution outside the US.

Gaps Tareeq can exploit

Student-facing experience is institutional, not consumer-grade. No emotional voice, no editorial onboarding, no mentor layer.

Tareeq takeaway: If selling into schools, Unifrog sets the depth bar for destination data. Out-compete by being the product the student actually wants to open — not the tool the counselor assigns.

YouScience

youscience.com
Aptitude assessment / schools

Audience

US schools (mostly), students 13–22.

What they sell

Brain-game style aptitude assessments that produce career and major matches, sold institutionally to schools.

Strengths

Differentiated assessment methodology, US school distribution, clean aptitude-to-career storyline.

Gaps Tareeq can exploit

US-centric, no regional fit, expensive institutional model, limited weekly-action layer post-assessment.

Tareeq takeaway: YouScience is the strongest schools-channel benchmark for assessment quality. Tareeq's 12-minute CORE assessment can match it on rigor while feeling like a product students choose to take, not one they're handed.

PathwayU

pathwayu.com
Purpose-fit assessment

Audience

US college students and higher-ed institutions.

What they sell

Purpose-led assessment (purpose, interests, workplace style) that matches to majors, careers, and degrees.

Strengths

The "purpose" framing resonates emotionally. Strong institution partnerships, polished outputs.

careerexplorer.com
Free consumer assessment + content funnel

Audience

Adults and teens, global English, primarily SEO-acquired.

What they sell

A long-form psychometric assessment + 1,000+ career profiles + premium content. Massive SEO content moat.

Strengths

SEO machine, career library breadth, conversion funnel from free assessment to paid features.

Gaps Tareeq can exploit

No regional fit. No local opportunity layer. Content is generic; experience is "fill out this form".

Tareeq takeaway: Model the top-of-funnel. Free, well-structured 12-minute assessment with SEO-ready career cluster pages. Then differentiate downstream with the "after" layer they don't ship.

Reading the field as a whole

Across the seven, the same shape repeats: an assessment, a result, a library, a paywall. The product surfaces all collapse into variations of the same noun — a test. Tareeq is the only one in this set that has the chance to be a path: something the user walks, not something the user takes.

04

What Tareeq should do differently.

tareeq · compass

The competitive set above hands the user a result and ends. Tareeq's product begins where they end. Six strategic differentiators define the wedge.

01 · Arabic-first, MENA-fluent

Bilingual EN/AR throughout, including TTS narration with Kai's voice in both. No competitor in this set ships Arabic-first. This alone defines a 50M+ youth audience moat.

02 · Family-aware framing

A parent-friendly summary of the user's CORE compass — written to be read aloud at the dinner table. The decision is the family's, not just the individual's.

03 · Local opportunity layer

Real internships, programs, university pathways, and scholarships in the user's city or country, surfaced contextually from their CORE result. Beats generic global career library.

04 · Mentor & counselor pairing

Not a chatbot — a human mentor warm-introduced based on cluster match. Counselor mode for schools mirrors the same flow.

05 · Weekly action steps

One actionable next step delivered each week post-assessment — an exploratory chat, a free course, a portfolio prompt. Turns a test into a habit.

06 · Compass, not GPS

Tareeq points the direction. The user chooses the destination. This editorial voice — already established on /intro and /contract — is the brand wedge no competitor matches.

Suggested MVP modules.

tareeq · compass

Six modules form a complete product surface. The first two are already shipping in production at tareeq-gray.vercel.app. The remaining four are the post-assessment arc — Tareeq's defensible territory and the part no competitor in this set delivers.

Shipping today

A · CORE assessment (12 min)

Foundation · live

54-question assessment across four pillars (Curiosities, Operations, Rewards, Ecosystems). Editorial onboarding with Kai, ritualistic Contract of Honesty, animated compass, milestone "Did you know" beats.

B · Compass result + persona

Foundation · next sprint

A named persona derived from the CORE pillars, framed as fit not score. Borrows Mentra's strengths-as-superpower framing for the local cultural context.

The defensible "after"

C · Local pathway library

Wedge · none of the 7 ship this

Programs, internships, scholarships, and university tracks within the user's country, filtered by CORE cluster. Seed with 5 anchor countries (KSA, UAE, Egypt, Jordan, Morocco).

D · Mentor & counselor pairing

Wedge · partial overlap with Yenza

A vetted human mentor matched on cluster + language + region. Counselor mode for schools mirrors the same flow for institutional buyers.

E · Weekly next step

Wedge · no competitor delivers cadence

One actionable next step per week post-assessment, tailored to cluster: a chat prompt, a free course, a portfolio task, a profile to message. Turns a one-time test into a 12-week habit.

F · Family / elder summary

Wedge · entirely unaddressed

A one-page printable or share-ready summary of the user's CORE compass and pathway — written for parents and elders, in their register. Removes the family conversation as a friction point.

Prioritization for Tareeq.

tareeq · compass

Sequenced by defensibility × effort. The first quadrant is what ships before fundraise; the rest define the 6–12 month roadmap.

Now — high impact, low effort

  • Ship the compass result + persona page (B).
  • Seed the local pathway library in 2 anchor countries (C, MVP slice).
  • Stand up the family-summary printable (F).
  • Bilingual EN/AR Kai voiceover on /intro and /q/* (locks the moat).

Next — high impact, higher effort

  • Mentor pairing v1 (D) — manual matching, 50 mentors.
  • Weekly next step push (E) — 12-week cluster-specific arc.
  • Extend pathway library (C) to 5 anchor countries.

Later — high impact, defer for scale

  • Counselor / schools mode (B2B variant of D + admin tooling).
  • Mentor marketplace (productize matching, vetting, payouts).
  • Localization beyond Arabic (Bahasa, Urdu).

Not yet — kill or postpone

  • Generic global career library (CareerExplorer territory).
  • University destination database depth (Unifrog territory).
  • Adult re-skilling lane (different ICP, different product).

The wedge in one sentence

The first Tareeq user to finish the assessment, get matched to a mentor in their city, receive a family-readable summary, and complete week one's action step — that's the moment no competitor in this set can replicate.
