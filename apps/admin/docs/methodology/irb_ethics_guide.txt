Ethics Approval Guide (IRB)
For Project Owner: Understanding Research Ethics Compliance

What is IRB/Ethics Approval?
IRB = Institutional Review Board (American term)
Ethics Committee = Same thing (more common internationally)
Simple explanation: Before you can use people’s data for research, an independent committee must review your plans and confirm you’re treating participants ethically.
Think of it like: A safety inspection before opening a restaurant. The health department checks that you’ll handle food safely. An ethics committee checks that you’ll handle human data safely.

Why You Need It
Legal Reason:
Many universities and journals won’t accept research without ethics approval. If you want to publish papers or get a PhD, you need this.
Practical Reason:
Protects you legally — Shows you followed proper procedures
Protects participants — Ensures their data is handled responsibly
Increases trust — Users feel safer when they know research is ethical
Enables partnerships — Universities require this before collaborating
What Happens Without It:
❌ Universities won’t collaborate with you
❌ Academic journals won’t publish your research
❌ PhD programs won’t accept your research data
❌ Potential legal issues if user privacy is violated

What Ethics Committees Review
The committee will ask:
1. What data are you collecting?
Question responses
Demographics (age, country, gender)
User behavior (time spent, device type)
2. How will you protect privacy?
Anonymization (user_id_hash, not real names)
Consent process (users opt-in explicitly)
Data storage (encrypted, secure servers)
3. What are the risks to participants?
Your project: Minimal risk (just taking a career assessment)
No physical harm
No psychological harm
No financial harm
4. How will you get consent?
Clear consent language (no jargon)
Voluntary participation (can decline or withdraw)
Age appropriate (minors handled correctly)
5. How will you store and share data?
Where is data stored? (AWS servers, encrypted)
Who has access? (Only research team)
How long kept? (Research data: permanent; personal data: deletable)
6. What are the benefits?
To participants: Better career guidance, helps future students
To society: Improved understanding of career development in MENA region

Types of Ethics Review
Exempt Review (Fastest, 1-2 weeks)
When it applies: Research poses minimal risk, uses anonymized data
Your project likely qualifies because: - No deception involved - No vulnerable populations (unless under 18) - Data is anonymized - Participants give informed consent
Process: Submit form → Committee chair reviews → Approved (or minor revisions)

Expedited Review (Medium, 2-4 weeks)
When it applies: More than minimal risk, but not full risk
Your project might need this if: - Collecting data from minors (under 18) - Asking sensitive questions (you’re not, but committees might be cautious) - Involving multiple countries (UAE/KSA/Egypt)
Process: Submit form → 1-2 committee members review → Approved (or minor revisions)

Full Review (Slowest, 4-8 weeks)
When it applies: Greater than minimal risk, vulnerable populations, experimental interventions
Your project probably doesn’t need this unless you’re doing something invasive.

If you’re not currently affiliated with a university, partner with one.

Pitch collaboration: “I’ve built a career assessment platform with 1000+ users. Would you be interested in co-authoring research papers?”
Submit through their university’s IRB — they guide you through the process

Option 2: Independent Ethics Review
If you can’t partner with a university, hire an independent review service.
Commercial IRB services: - WCG IRB (wirb.com) — International, handles commercial research - Pearl IRB (pearl-irb.com) — US-based but works internationally - Advarra (advarra.com) — Commercial review board
Process:
Contact service
Pay fee ($500-2000 USD depending on complexity)
Submit application
Review (2-4 weeks)
Approved → You can collect data
Pros: - Faster than university committees - No need for academic partnership - Professional and recognized
Cons: - Costs money - Less credible than university IRB for PhD purposes - Some journals prefer university-based approval
Timeline: 2-4 weeks
Cost: $500-2000 USD

Option 3: Wait Until PhD Enrollment
If you’re enrolling in a PhD program soon, wait and get approval through your PhD university.
Process:
Enroll in PhD program (Month 12 of your roadmap)
Submit IRB application through PhD university
Get approval
Retrospectively apply to data already collected (with consent)
Pros: - Free - PhD advisor helps you - Strongest academic credibility
Cons: - Can’t formally use Phase 1 data for research until this is done - Delays research publications - Some committees won’t approve retrospective studies
Timeline: 4-8 weeks after PhD enrollment

Risks to participants: Minimal
No physical risk
No psychological distress (it’s a career assessment, not therapy)
Possible risk: User sees result they don’t like (mitigated by framing as “guidance” not “destiny”)
Benefits to participants:
Immediate: Personalized career guidance (worth $20-50 if paid)
Long-term: Better career decisions, reduced uncertainty
Societal: Contributes to research improving career guidance regionally

5. Consent Process
How consent is obtained:
Users read consent form before starting assessment
Must check “I consent” box to proceed
Can decline research participation and still get results
Can withdraw consent later via account settings
Consent form language:
Written at 8th-grade reading level (age-appropriate)
Available in English and Arabic
Explains data use clearly (no legal jargon)
Special considerations for minors (<18):
In UAE/KSA/Egypt, parental consent may be required depending on country law
MVP: Users under 18 are excluded from research (can still use platform)
Phase 2: Add parental consent system if needed

6. Privacy and Confidentiality
How data is protected:
Anonymization: user_id is hashed (one-way, cannot reverse)
Storage: Encrypted database on secure servers (AWS Middle East region)
Access: Only research team (you + academic partners)
Sharing: Only with explicit user consent (for university partnerships)
Retention: Personal data deletable; research data permanent but anonymized
No identifiers in research: Published papers use participant IDs, not names

7. Data Management Plan
Where data is stored:
Production database: AWS RDS (Middle East region)
Research exports: Encrypted CSV files on secure university servers
Backup: AWS S3 with encryption
Who has access:
Project owner (you)
Academic collaborators (co-authors)
PhD advisors (if applicable)
NOT accessible to:
Marketing teams
Third-party advertisers
Anyone outside research team
Data retention:
Research data: Permanent (required for replication studies)
Personal data (email, name): Deletable on user request
Assessment results: Permanent (anonymized)

Attach to IRB application:

FAQ: Common Questions
Q: Do I need approval in all three countries (UAE, KSA, Egypt)?
A: Ideally, yes. But practically for MVP: - Start with one: Get approval from one university (e.g., AUS in UAE) - Expand later: Add other countries in Phase 2 - Or use international IRB: Some commercial IRBs cover multiple countries

Q: What if I can’t find a university partner?
A: Use commercial IRB (Option 2). It’s legitimate and journals accept it.

Q: Does this delay my MVP launch?
A: It can. Two strategies: 1. Wait for approval (2-6 weeks) then launch — cleanest approach 2. Launch first (collect data with consent), apply for approval in Month 3-6 — faster but riskier

Q: Can I use data collected before approval?
A: Maybe. If users consented at the time (“I agree my data can be used for research”), some committees allow it. But not guaranteed.
Safest: Get approval before collecting data.

Q: What if the committee requests changes?
A: Common. They might ask you to: - Revise consent language (make it clearer) - Add parental consent for minors - Specify data retention period - Clarify who has data access
Solution: Make requested changes and resubmit (usually fast, 1 week).

Q: Do I need approval every year?
A: Usually need renewal annually. Submit brief update: - How many participants so far? - Any problems or complaints? - Any changes to procedures?
Renewal is faster than initial approval (1-2 weeks).
